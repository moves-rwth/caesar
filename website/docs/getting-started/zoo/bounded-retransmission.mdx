A sender retries a packet if no acknowledgement arrives.
The *bounded retransmission protocol* gives each packet a limited number of transmission attempts <Citation {...references.protocol} href="https://www.cs.ru.nl/~herman/PUBS/NijmegenTypes.pdf#page=183" locator={undefined} />.
If all attempts for one packet fail, the sender abandons the remaining packets.

How likely is success, and how many attempts are made on average?
We first bound message failure and failed attempts in a complete retry loop, then examine the probability and cost of retrying an individual packet.
The [optional final section](#an-abstract-model-of-packet-delivery) studies packet sequences that summarize each packet attempt by one probabilistic step.

## A Complete Message in One Loop {#combined-message}

The following model sends `packets` packets, allowing at most `maxTries` transmission attempts per packet <Citation {...references.toolpaper} />.
Each attempt independently succeeds with probability $0.99$.
Success means that the packet arrives and its acknowledgement returns.

### Probability of Abandoning the Message {#message-failure}

For `maxTries > 0`, we bound the probability of exhausting a packet's retry limit.

<ExampleSources exampleId="bounded-retransmission" sources={[{
  variantId: 'combined', code: combinedSource, downloadUrl: combinedDownload,
  title: 'Complete message: failure probability',
  start: '@wp\ncoproc brp(', end: '\n@wp\ncoproc brp_failed_transmissions(',
  highlight: '4-6,13-18,23-27',
}]} />

After each successful transmission, `sent` increases and `fail` resets to zero, giving the next packet its full retry allowance.
The counter `totalFail` keeps failures across all packets.
At exit, `fail == maxTries` means that the sender abandoned the message.
The loop makes at most `packets * maxTries` transmissions, so it always terminates.

One packet fails all $m=\mathtt{maxTries}$ attempts with probability $0.01^m$.
Successfully sending all $n=\mathtt{packets}$ packets therefore has probability $(1-0.01^m)^n$.
The `coproc` specification verifies the complementary upper bound

$$
\Pr(\mathtt{fail}=m)\le 1-\bigl(1-0.01^m\bigr)^n.
$$

For `packets == 0`, no transmission is made and the bound is zero.

The invariant uses probability one when the current packet has exhausted its attempts and zero when all packets have been sent.
While transmission continues, the factor `1 - pow(0.01, maxTries - fail)` is the current packet's success probability within its remaining attempts.
The factor `pow(1 - pow(0.01, maxTries), (packets - sent) - 1)` covers all later packets.
Their product gives the success probability for the whole message.
Subtracting it from one gives the remaining failure probability.

### Expected Number of Failed Attempts {#message-failed-transmissions}

The next procedure bounds the expected number of failed attempts across the whole message.
The excerpt shows its specification and invariant for the same transmission loop.

<ExampleSources exampleId="bounded-retransmission" sources={[{
  variantId: 'combined', code: combinedSource, downloadUrl: combinedDownload,
  title: 'Complete message: count failed attempts',
  start: '@wp\ncoproc brp_failed_transmissions(', end: '\n    while sent < packets',
  highlight: '4-5,13-16',
}]} />

Caesar verifies

$$
\mathbb{E}[\mathtt{totalFail}]\le 0.01\cdot m\cdot n.
$$

The counter includes failed attempts before a later success.
The bound allows all $n\cdot m$ transmission slots, although successful delivery or abandoning the message can leave some unused.
It also applies when either input is zero.

The invariant retains `totalFail` and adds an allowance of $0.01$ for each remaining transmission slot.
The term `maxTries * (packets - sent) - fail` removes slots already consumed on the current packet.
A failed transmission consumes one slot and increments the counter; a success can discard the unused slots of that packet.
The expected counter increase is therefore covered by the decrease in the allowance.

## Packet-Level Retry Proofs {#retrying-one-packet}

The following variants isolate the retry loop for one packet, then combine these retries in a nested message loop.
The transmission probability `p()` and retry limit `MAX()` are symbolic <Citation {...references.thesis} locator="§4.1" />.

<ExecutionDiagram kind="packet-retries">

Each transmission succeeds independently with probability `p()`, where $0\le\mathtt{p}()\le1$.
The procedure `sendPacket` stops after the first success or after `MAX()` failures, with `MAX()` a nonnegative integer.
With `MAX() == 0`, it makes no attempt and returns `success == false`.

</ExecutionDiagram>

### Success Probability for One Packet {#packet-success}

Caesar verifies a success probability of at least `packet_success()`, defined as `1 - pow(1 - p(), MAX())`.

<ExampleSources exampleId="bounded-retransmission" sources={[{variantId: "success", code: successSource, downloadUrl: successDownload, title: "Send one packet: success probability", start: "@wlp\nproc sendPacket(", end: "@wlp\nproc BRP(", highlight: "3-4,10-12"}]} />

The loop makes at most `MAX()` attempts, so the [`@wlp` annotation](../../proof-rules/approximations.md#calculus-annotations) gives a bound on success probability without requiring a separate termination proof.
The [`@invariant` annotation](../../proof-rules/induction.md) uses probability one after success and `1 - pow(1 - p(), MAX() - failed)` for success within the remaining attempts.
With $m>0$ attempts remaining and $p=\mathtt{p}()$, its preservation follows from

$$
p+(1-p)\cdot\bigl(1-(1-p)^{m-1}\bigr)=1-(1-p)^m.
$$

If all attempts fail, the bound is zero.

<span id="sending-a-sequence-with-retries" />

### Success Probability for All Packets {#success-probability}

The procedure `BRP` applies the retry loop to each of $N$ packets, stopping when all succeed or one uses all its attempts without success.
Caesar verifies a probability of at least $\mathtt{packet\_success}()^N$ for successfully sending the whole sequence.

<ExampleSources exampleId="bounded-retransmission" sources={[{variantId: "success", code: successSource, downloadUrl: successDownload, title: "Send all packets: nested retry annotations", start: "@wlp\nproc BRP(", highlight: "3-4,10-12,17-20"}]} />

The outer invariant uses `pow(packet_success(), N - sent)` for all unsent packets and becomes zero after a packet fails.
The inner invariant multiplies the current packet's success bound by `pow(packet_success(), (N - sent) - 1)` for all later packets.

There are at most $N$ packet attempts, each with at most `MAX()` transmissions, so the loop terminates.
For $N=0$, no packet is attempted and `success` remains true.

For `p() == 0.99`, `MAX() == maxTries`, and `N == packets`, this success bound is the complement of the failure bound in the [single-loop model](#message-failure).

### Expected Number of Transmission Attempts {#expected-iterations}

When `p() > 0`, Caesar verifies that the expected number of transmission attempts for one packet is at most `MAX() ⊓ (1 / p())`.

<ExampleSources exampleId="bounded-retransmission" sources={[{variantId: "termination", code: terminationSource, downloadUrl: terminationDownload, title: "sendPacket: expected number of transmission attempts", start: "@ert\ncoproc sendPacket(", end: "\n// Count packet attempts", highlight: "3-4,10,17"}]} />

With the [`@ert` annotation](../../proof-rules/approximations.md#calculus-annotations), one `reward 1` per transmission makes the expected cost count transmission attempts.
At most `MAX() - failed` further attempts are possible.
Writing $p=\mathtt{p}()$, the expected number of attempts until success without a retry limit is $1/p$, since

$$
1+(1-p)\cdot\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p}.
$$

The invariant bounds the remaining attempts by both quantities, and success ends the wait.
With `MAX() == 0`, the bound is zero.

### Expected Number of Failed Attempts {#transmission-counts}

Caesar verifies that the expected number of unsuccessful transmission attempts, recorded by `failed`, is at most `MAX() * (1 - p())`.

<ExampleSources exampleId="bounded-retransmission" sources={[{variantId: "failed", code: failedSource, downloadUrl: failedDownload, title: "sendPacket: specification and invariant for failed transmissions", start: "@wp\ncoproc sendPacket(", end: "\n// Bound the expected value of totalFailed", highlight: "3-4,10-12,17"}]} />

While retrying, `(1 - p()) * (MAX() - failed)` bounds further failures by allowing every remaining transmission, even though success can stop the loop earlier.
At exit, the invariant equals `failed`.

## Optional: Abstract Packet Sequences {#an-abstract-model-of-packet-delivery}

The remaining `BRP` procedures summarize each packet attempt by one `flip(s())`, without tracking individual retries <Citation {...references.thesis} locator="§§2.4, 4.1" />.
The packet success probability satisfies $0\le\mathtt{s}()\le1$ and is not related to `p()` or `MAX()` in these files.

These procedures stop after $N$ successful packet attempts or the first failure.
Thus, they make at most $N$ packet attempts, with no attempt when $N=0$.

### Expected Number of Packet Attempts {#expected-packet-attempts}

When `s() < 1`, Caesar verifies that the expected number of packet attempts is at most `N ⊓ (1 / (1 - s()))`.

<ExampleSources exampleId="bounded-retransmission" sources={[{variantId: "termination", code: terminationSource, downloadUrl: terminationDownload, title: "Abstract sequence: expected packet attempts", start: "@ert\ncoproc BRP(", highlight: "3-4,10,17"}]} />

Here one `reward 1` counts a whole packet attempt, including an unsuccessful final attempt.
At most `N - sent` further packets can be attempted.
Writing $s=\mathtt{s}()$, waiting for the first failed packet attempt with no packet limit has expected cost $1/(1-s)$.
One attempt adds one reward unit and continues with probability `s()`, so preservation uses $1+s/(1-s)=1/(1-s)$.
Completing all packets can stop the sequence sooner.
In particular, the bound is zero for $N=0$.

### Expected Number of Sent Packets {#delivered-packets}

Caesar verifies that the expected number of successfully sent packets, recorded by `sent`, is at most `N * s()`.

<ExampleSources exampleId="bounded-retransmission" sources={[{variantId: "sent", code: sentSource, downloadUrl: sentDownload, title: "BRP: specification and invariant for sent packets", start: "@wp\ncoproc BRP(", highlight: "3-4,10-12,17-19"}]} />

The term `s() * (N - sent)` bounds further successes by allowing all remaining packet attempts, even though the first failure stops the sequence.

### Fixed Cost per Packet Attempt {#accumulated-cost}

In `failed.heyvl`, each packet attempt adds the fixed nonnegative integer `f()` to `totalFailed`, including an unsuccessful attempt.
The file does not relate `f()` to individual transmission failures.
Caesar verifies that the expected value of this counter is at most `N * f()`.

<ExampleSources exampleId="bounded-retransmission" sources={[{variantId: "failed", code: failedSource, downloadUrl: failedDownload, title: "Abstract sequence: accumulate f() per packet attempt", start: "@wp\ncoproc BRP(", highlight: "3-4,11-12,15,17,20"}]} />

Every packet attempt either increments `sent` or stops the sequence, so `f() * (N - sent)` covers all remaining cost.
