A random walk can move away from its target, so progress made on one step may be lost on the next.
Our first program moves two places toward a target or one place away, using a fair coin to choose the direction.

We bound the expected number of flips needed to reach the target, then ask whether related walks terminate with probability one when choices are adversarial or steps depend on parity.

## Positive Drift {#positive-drift-cost}

The procedure `drift_walk` starts at `init_x` and stops when `x >= init_n`.
A fair coin flip increases `x` by two or decreases it by one.
At zero, the downward step leaves `x` unchanged.

With the [`@ert` annotation](../../proof-rules/approximations.md#calculus-annotations), each `tick 1` counts one flip.
The `coproc` specification bounds the expected number of flips from above by `2 * ((init_n + 1) - init_x)`.

The [`@invariant` annotation](../../proof-rules/induction.md) supplies `I = 2 * ((n + 1) - x)`.
The `n + 1` in `I` allows for a final step to overshoot the target by one.
The expected cost including the tick is at most

$$
1+\tfrac12\cdot(I-4)+\tfrac12\cdot(I+2)=I.
$$

At exit, $I\ge0$ bounds `post 0`.

## Demonic Choice {#demonic-walk-termination}

The *demonically fair walk*, implemented by `demonic_walk`, starts at `init_x` and stops at zero.
A fair coin decides whether to decrease `x` by one or make a demonic choice between increasing it by one and staying put.

With `pre 1`, `post 1`, and the [`@ast` annotation](../../proof-rules/ast.md), Caesar proves almost-sure termination under every resolution of the demonic choice.

The variant `x` decreases by one with probability $1/2$.
Its expected value is at most

$$
\tfrac12\cdot(x-1)+\tfrac12\cdot(x+1)=x.
$$

Choosing to stay put gives a smaller expectation, so either demonic choice satisfies the expected-value condition.

This walk also models a recursive process in which `x` counts pending calls <Citation {...references.termination} />.
A call either returns or is replaced by one or two further calls, with the number of new calls chosen demonically.

## Parity-Dependent Steps {#parity-walk-termination}

The final walk uses different steps at even and odd positions.
The procedure `parity_walk` starts at any nonnegative integer `init_x` and stops at ten.

At even positions it moves down or up by two with equal probability.
At odd positions it moves up by one without flipping a coin.

Caesar again proves almost-sure termination with `pre 1`, `post 1`, and the [`@ast` annotation](../../proof-rules/ast.md).

- **Odd positions:** the term `3 * ite(x % 2 != 0, 1, 0)` compensates for a step away from ten.
  It drops by three at the next even position, so the variant decreases by at least two.
- **Even positions:** a decrease by two has probability at least $1/2$, and the expected variant does not increase.
