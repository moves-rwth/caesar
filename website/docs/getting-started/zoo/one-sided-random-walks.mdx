Obtaining several successes from repeated independent trials may take arbitrarily many attempts.
Our countdown models this with a fair coin: a counter records how many heads are still needed, decreasing on heads and staying unchanged on tails <Citation {...references.termination} locator={undefined} />.

Does the countdown reach zero with probability one, and is the expected number of flips finite?
We prove both properties, then derive numerical bounds for two related walks.

## Countdown with Pauses {#countdown-termination}

<ExecutionDiagram kind="countdown">

The procedure `countdown` starts at the nonnegative integer `init_x` and stops at zero, when all required heads have occurred.
Each iteration makes one coin flip.

</ExecutionDiagram>

### Almost-Sure Termination {#countdown-ast}

With `pre 1`, `post 1`, and the [`@ast` annotation](../../proof-rules/ast.md), Caesar proves that the countdown reaches zero with probability one.
The [`@wp` annotation](../../proof-rules/approximations.md#calculus-annotations) selects the calculus in which `post 1` measures the termination probability.

<ExampleSources exampleId="one-sided-random-walks" sources={[
  {
    variantId: 'ast', code: astSource, downloadUrl: astDownload,
    title: 'Almost-sure termination annotation',
    start: '@wp\nproc countdown(', highlight: '1,3-4,10',

]} />

The variant is `x`.
For $x>0$, one iteration decreases it by one with probability $1/2$, and its expected value is

$$
\tfrac12\cdot(x-1)+\tfrac12\cdot x=x-\tfrac12\le x.
$$

These establish the progress and expected-value conditions of the almost-sure termination rule.

### Positive Almost-Sure Termination {#past-finite-expected-number-of-coin-flips}

The countdown charges `reward 1` per flip, and Caesar verifies that the expected number of flips is at most `3 * init_x + 1`.
The [`@past` annotation](../../proof-rules/past.md) proves positive almost-sure termination and supplies this upper bound for the `@ert coproc`.

<ExampleSources exampleId="one-sided-random-walks" sources={[
  {
    variantId: 'past', code: pastSource, downloadUrl: pastDownload,
    title: 'Positive almost-sure termination and an upper bound on flips',
    start: '@ert\ncoproc countdown(', end: '\n        var step: Bool', highlight: '1,3-4,9,11',
  },
]} />

On heads, the ranking superinvariant `3 * x + 1` drops by $3$.
After charging for the flip, its expected decrease is $\tfrac12\cdot 3 - 1 = \tfrac12$.
It is above the threshold $K=1$ while the loop is active and equals $K$ at exit.

## Expected Number of Coin Flips to Reach a Target {#forward-walk-cost}

We now count up to a target instead of counting down to zero.
The procedure `forward_walk` starts at `init_x` and stops when `x >= init_n`.
Each fair coin flip either increases `x` by one or leaves it unchanged.

With the [`@ert` annotation](../../proof-rules/approximations.md#calculus-annotations), each `tick 1` counts one flip.
The `coproc` specification bounds the expected number of flips by `2 * (init_n - init_x)`.
Subtraction on `UInt` stops at zero, so the bound is zero if the target has already been reached.

<ExampleSources exampleId="one-sided-random-walks" sources={[
  {
    variantId: 'expected-cost', code: costSource, downloadUrl: costDownload,
    title: 'Expected number of coin flips: specification and invariant',
    start: '@ert\ncoproc forward_walk(', highlight: '1,3-4,9',
  },
]} />

The [`@invariant` annotation](../../proof-rules/induction.md) supplies `I = 2 * (n - x)` as a bound on the expected number of coin flips still needed.
Its expected decrease covers the current `tick 1`:

$$
1+\tfrac12\cdot(I-2)+\tfrac12\cdot I=I.
$$

At exit the invariant is zero, matching `post 0`.

## Expected Number of Coin Flips with Steps of One or Two {#two-step-countdown-cost}

The final countdown has no pauses: a biased coin flip decreases `x` by one with probability $1/3$ and by two with probability $2/3$.
It stops when `x` is zero or one, and each flip still counts as one tick.

The `coproc` specification bounds the expected number of flips by $0.6\cdot\mathtt{init\_x}$.

<ExampleSources exampleId="one-sided-random-walks" sources={[
  {
    variantId: 'two-step', code: twoStepSource, downloadUrl: twoStepDownload,
    title: 'Expected number of coin flips: specification and invariant',
    start: '@ert\ncoproc countdown(', highlight: '1,3-4,10',
  },
]} />

The `@invariant` annotation supplies $I(x)=0.6\cdot x$, which covers the tick because

$$
1+\tfrac13\cdot I(x-1)+\tfrac23\cdot I(x-2)=I(x)
\qquad(x\ge2).
$$

At exit, $I(x)\ge0$ bounds `post 0`.
