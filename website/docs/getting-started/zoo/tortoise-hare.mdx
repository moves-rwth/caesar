<ExampleMetadata exampleId="tortoise-hare" />

A tortoise advances one unit per round, while a hare flips a fair coin to decide whether to move.
When the hare moves, its jump is sampled uniformly from the continuous interval $[0,10]$.
The race ends when the hare is ahead after both have moved <Citation {...references.continuous} locator={undefined} />.
We want an upper bound on the expected final round count.

:::warning Reusing this encoding

Caesar checks the encoding, whose soundness relies on the [conditions below](#soundness-conditions).
Check these conditions when adapting the encoding to another continuous program.

:::

## The Program {#the-program-and-its-specification}

The program starts at positions `init_h` and `init_t`, counts completed rounds in `count`, and approximates the hare's continuous jumps using eight intervals.

<ExampleSources exampleId="tortoise-hare" sources={[{
  variantId: 'main', code: source, downloadUrl: download,
  title: 'Race with an upper approximation of continuous sampling',
  start: '@wp', highlight: '3-4,11,19-21,23-24',
}]} />

For `init_h <= init_t`, the `pre` clause bounds the expected final `count` by `3.38 * ((init_t - init_h) + 2)`.
If the hare starts ahead, the loop is skipped and the count is zero.
Under [`@wp`](../../proof-rules/approximations.md#calculus-annotations), nonterminating runs contribute zero, so this bound does not prove termination.

## Continuous Sampling {#approximating-the-continuous-move}

<TortoiseHareJump>

Suppose the hare starts a round at 0 and the tortoise moves from 2 to 3.
The hare must jump more than 3 units to end the race.
The highlighted interval $[2.5,3.75]$ contains jumps that lead to both outcomes.

</TortoiseHareJump>

The hare's jump is ten times a draw from $[0,1]$.
The encoding divides that interval into eight equal parts.

<ExampleSourceExcerpt source={source.replace(/^ {12}/gm, '')} start="var inc: UReal" end={'\n        } else {}'} title="Upper Riemann approximation of the hare's jump" />

The [`unif(0, 7)`](../../stdlib/distributions.md#uniform) draw chooses each interval with probability $1/8$.
Then [`cohavoc`](../../heyvl/statements.md#havoc) and [`coassume`](../../heyvl/statements.md#assumptions) take the supremum over all values in the selected interval.
For a measurable expectation $f$, this gives the upper sum

$$
\int_0^1 f(u)\,du
\le \frac18\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{7}
\sup_{u\in[j/8,(j+1)/8]} f(u).
$$

The supremum accounts for every possible move within an interval, including jumps that change whether the race continues.

## Soundness Conditions {#soundness-conditions}

The paper's source programs use the following constructs, expressed here in HeyVL notation <Citation {...references.continuous} href="https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19388v1#S4.SS1" locator="Definition 4.1" />:

- Assignments to `UReal` variables, sequential composition, `if`, and `while`.
- Probabilistic choice, written as `flip(p)` followed by `if`, with fixed rational $p\in[0,1]$.
- Continuous uniform draws from $[0,1]$ using the encoding above, and hard observations written as `assert ?(b)`.

Assignments, guards, and postexpectations must be measurable.
The race meets this requirement through polynomial arithmetic and comparisons, with postexpectation `count`.

## Verification {#bounding-the-remaining-rounds}

Let $I$ denote the expectation in the [`@invariant` annotation](../../proof-rules/induction.md).
While `h <= t`, its term `3.38 * ((t - h) + 2)` bounds the expected rounds still to come, in addition to those already counted.

Let $C$ be the original continuous loop and $B$ its body after the sampling encoding above.
Caesar checks the following inequality whenever `h <= t`:

$$
\operatorname{vp}\llbracket B\rrbracket(I)\le I.
$$

At loop exit, $I=\mathtt{count}$, giving the required postexpectation.
The factor `3.38` satisfies these checks with eight intervals <Citation {...references.continuous} href="https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19388v1#S9.T2" locator="Table 2" />.

The paper's upper-bound theorem <Citation {...references.continuous} href="https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19388v1#S6" locator="Theorem 6.2(1)" /> then gives

$$
\operatorname{wp}\llbracket C\rrbracket(\mathtt{count})\le I.
$$
